Breeze is an AI copilot for neighbourhood heat action. It fuses open satellite, tree-canopy and census data into a ranked heat-equity map, an optimised cooling budget, and alerts that reach the people most at risk — in minutes, not months.

Why this, why now

Heat is not evenly distributed. Neither is the response.

City cooling budgets are small and time is short. The hard part is not measuring heat — it is deciding who gets help first, and being able to defend that decision.

03 / 关怀视图

比路线更重要的，是名单

同一个热模型，反过来用：不是给人找路，而是给社区工作者排今天的敲门顺序。

社区网格员每天只有 3 小时能出门。凉径的热模型把楼栋、时段、人口结构叠在一起，输出一份按热风险排序的走访清单——把有限的人力放在最可能出事的地方。

名单不是判决书：每一条都附了可执行的动作，和一句「为什么是它」。

04 / 方法与边界

它能做什么，不能做什么

评审标准里权重最高的两条是「气候相关性」和「AI 集成」，但最容易失分的是把原型说成产品。所以这一节写得比前面都直白。

数据

  • 演示用合成街道网络 + 简化地表热模型：T = T_air + ΔT·(1−shade)
  • 真实部署接 Landsat 8/9 TIRS、ECOSTRESS 地表温度产品 + 街景影像 + 社区气象站
  • 人口脆弱度用社区授权的匿名统计口径

AI 做了什么

  • 视觉分割：街景中提取树冠、铺装、遮阳设施 → 遮荫系数
  • 路径优化：以「累计热暴露」为目标函数的多目标最短路，而非最短距离
  • LLM：把数值结果改写成老人听得懂的一句话建议

局限

  • 热模型未用实测标定，绝对温度不可信，只有相对比较有意义
  • 体感温度未纳入湿度、风速、个体生理差异
  • 尚未做真实用户测试，路线收益是模型估计而非实测

为什么仍然是它

  • 问题足够具体：一段 400 米的路、一群走不快的人
  • AI 的位置不可替代：阴影识别无法靠人工标注覆盖全城
  • 失败也能交付：名单和遮荫数据本身就是社区资产
科学依据公式是简化版，但每一项都对应可查证的公开研究
热应激指标

WBGT（ISO 7243）与 UTCI 是国际通用的户外热应激指标。本原型的「平均体感」是它们的简化代理。

遮荫效应

美国环保署（EPA）实测：树荫下材料表面峰值温度比无遮荫低 11–25℃。模型中 shade 系数的量级来源于此。

城市热制图

SOLWEIG 等模型已用街景几何 + 气象数据逐点计算平均辐射温度；Landsat 8/9 与 ECOSTRESS 提供地表温度产品。

路径目标

以累计热暴露 ∫T·dt 最小化代替最短距离，是步行热暴露研究中的常用目标函数。

现实先例

巴黎「清凉岛」地图与新加坡 Cooling Singapore 计划，都在把热舒适做成可查询、可规划的公共服务。

评审速览

30% · 气候相关性与潜在影响

聚焦极端高温下的适应问题，用户是能指名道姓的：独居老人、骑手、婴幼儿家庭。影响路径可算：减少的户外热暴露分钟数 × 人数。

20% · AI 应用与集成

三个环节各有其职：视觉分割产生遮荫数据、优化算法生成路径、LLM 负责把数值翻译成人话。第 04 节主动列出了模型失效场景。

20% · 创新性与创造力

不把热风险做成又一张热力图，而是做成一个决策：走哪条路、先敲谁的门。同一个模型服务两种角色。

20% · 原型实现

单文件可运行，时间轴、角色、图层、路线均可交互，无需登录与网络。屏幕共享即可演示，3 分钟内讲完核心闭环。

10% · 展示与路演

建议演示顺序：拖动时间轴到 20:00，让 AI 自己推翻推荐——「晚上八点，这 121 米不值得多走」。这是全篇最诚实的一秒。

04 · Method & limitations

Where the AI helps — and where it stops.

Judges and city partners should be able to audit the reasoning. Breeze separates evidence, allocation and communication into three explainable layers.

Layer 1 · Evidence

Open data in, ranked risk out

Satellite land-surface temperature, canopy cover, facilities and census grids are joined at block level. No proprietary data required, so any city can rerun it.

  • Landsat 9 TIRS — 30 m surface temperature
  • Sentinel-2 NDVI — tree canopy proxy
  • OpenStreetMap — cooling facilities
  • Gridded census — age and income shares
Layer 2 · Decision

A score a council can defend

Heat, vulnerability, canopy deficit and population exposure are combined with fixed, published weights. The optimizer then buys the largest risk reduction per dollar.

risk = 0.40·heat + 0.35·vulnerability
    + 0.15·canopy deficit + 0.10·exposure
  • 1.6× weight for the most vulnerable quartile
  • Every allocation shows cost, °C relief, people reached
  • Human can override any line item
Layer 3 · Communication

AI writes, humans send

The language layer turns rankings into short, plain-language alerts per audience and per language. It is deliberately templated in this prototype.

  • English, Chinese and Turkish outputs
  • Audience-specific actions, not generic advice
  • Reviewed by a community health worker
  • Every message points to a cool space

What the AI does / does not do

  • Does: rank blocks, quantify trade-offs, draft and translate messages, surface equity gaps that manual planning misses.
  • Does not: replace field validation, measure individual heat strain, claim causal impact, or decide who receives help without human review.
  • Does not use: personal data, mobility traces or household identifiers. Targeting stays at neighbourhood aggregate level.
No personal data Human-in-the-loop Auditable weights Open inputs

Limitations & next validation

  • Simulated demo: 24 illustrative blocks and 48.8k residents. All temperatures, costs and effects are synthetic.
  • Weights are priors: must be calibrated against ambulance dispatches and heat-related emergency visits.
  • Next pilot: two weeks with one municipality; validate surface temperature against 10 low-cost sensors; A/B test alert formats with community health workers.
  • Open by design: publish the scoring spec so other cities can reuse and challenge it.
